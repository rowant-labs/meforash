# Large-model comparison: prospective review notes

Prepared 2026-09-05 (local project date), before inspecting responses to these eight new cases. These are AI source checks and proposed review criteria, **not human-approved gold answers**. Preserve room for a qualified reviewer to correct a parsing or accept another defensible translation. Evaluate the main answer and any added claims separately: a correct translation does not excuse an invented citation.

The [dataset manifest](comparison-large-v1-manifest.json) records the exact source files, excerpts, dataset hash, and unchanged first 16 rows. All eight new passage selections are absent from the original pilot's `source_refs`; they remain development questions. Prior model exposure to these biblical passages is unknown. Only the question and original-language excerpt are submitted to models; criteria and these notes are excluded from prompts and training. The original biblical passages may occur in whole-corpus adaptation.

OSHB excerpts retain direct main/ketiv word elements, omitting notes and joining words after removing morpheme slashes. Greek excerpts preserve the pinned SBLGNT verse wording and editorial marks. Neither source is a diplomatic transcription of an original manuscript. OSHB morphology below follows its [code definitions](https://hb.openscriptures.org/parsing/HebrewMorphologyCodes.html); source annotations are evidence to assess, not infallible judgments.

| Case | Source-checked findings to assess | Material mistakes to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| H13 · Ruth 2:8 | `תֵּלְכִי` is tagged `HVqj2fs`: a Qal second-person feminine singular jussive with the negative particle `אַל`. An explanation as the imperfect form used in a prohibition is acceptable. `לִלְקֹט` is `HR/Vqc`, preposition plus Qal infinitive construct. Ruth is told not to go glean in another field or leave here, and to remain with Boaz's young women. | Calling both expressions infinitives; changing the addressee's number or gender; treating the prohibition as a general ban on gleaning. |
| H14 · 2 Kings 6:21–23 | Verse 21 identifies the king's question. In verse 22, `תַכֶּה` is Hiphil imperfect second masculine singular and `שִׂים` Qal imperative second masculine singular. The prohibition and command address the king. Plural suffixes and third masculine plural eating/drinking/departure verbs concern the captured people. The question about captives taken with sword and bow is rhetorical. Verse 23 narrates provision, eating, drinking, and release. | Making the prisoners feed the king; losing the prohibition; treating the rhetorical question as an additional command to kill. “Master” versus “masters” should be assessed in context rather than mechanically scored from a plural noun alone. |
| H15 · 1 Samuel 1:12–15 | Hannah continues praying; Eli watches her mouth. Her lips move, but her voice is not heard. `יִשָּׁמֵעַ` is `HVNi3ms`, Niphal imperfect third masculine singular, agreeing with her voice. Eli reckons her drunk and speaks accordingly. Hannah denies drinking wine or strong drink and explains her distress and prayer. | Translating the passive as Hannah being unable to hear; reporting Eli's conclusion as the narrator's finding; overlooking Hannah's explicit denial. |
| A13 · Ezra 4:21 | `שִׂימוּ` is Peal imperative second masculine plural; `לְבַטָּלָא` is preposition plus Pael infinitive construct. `תִתְבְּנֵא` is tagged `AVui3fs`, passive Hithpeel imperfect agreeing with the city; `יִתְּשָׂם` is `AVui3ms`, passive imperfect agreeing with the order. Hithpeel/Ithpeel terminology may differ by grammar. The addressees must stop these men, and the city is not to be rebuilt until an order from the speaker is issued. | Calling the two finite passives infinitives, reversing who authorizes the work, or treating “until” as a permanent unconditional prohibition. Avoid demanding historical claims about a particular decree beyond what the supplied sentence can establish. |
| A14 · Ezra 6:7–8 | `שְׁבֻקוּ` is second masculine plural imperative; `יִבְנוֹן` is Peal imperfect third masculine plural. The Jewish governor and elders build the house on its site. Verse 8 supplies expenses from royal revenue associated with tribute beyond the River. `תֶּהֱוֵא` is Peal imperfect third feminine singular, with passive participle `מִתְיַהֲבָא`, agreeing with the expense. The closing negative plus Pael infinitive concerns avoiding cessation/interruption. | Turning support into an order to stop rebuilding; making the elders finance it from their own resources; reversing the passive payment relationship. English “without interruption,” “so the work does not stop,” or another justified rendering can be acceptable; the implied subject of the final infinitive deserves explanation if asserted precisely. |
| A15 · Daniel 6:11, supplied MT numbering | `בָּרֵךְ` is Peal active participle masculine singular, here kneeling; `מְצַלֵּא` Pael active participle; `מוֹדֵא` Aphel active participle. `הֲוָא` is Peal perfect third masculine singular and `עָבֵד` Peal active participle. Daniel prays and gives thanks three times daily, as he had done previously. The verse describes the windows as open; it does not narrate a newly performed opening. | Calling kneeling a command to bless; calling all forms finite imperfects or infinitives; claiming the prayer practice began in response to the decree. Source numbering is printed explicitly; assess the supplied verse rather than penalizing a responsibly identified edition-numbering difference. |
| G13 · 2 Thessalonians 3:10–12 | `θέλει` is present active indicative third singular; `ἐργάζεσθαι` present middle infinitive; `ἐσθιέτω` present active imperative third singular. The condition says that someone is unwilling to work, followed by “let that person not eat either.” The surrounding sentences concern disorderly conduct, not working but meddling, and quiet work to eat one's own bread. | Substituting physical inability or unemployment for unwillingness; presenting the imperative as a prediction of what will happen; asserting that these verses alone settle every modern assistance policy. |
| G14 · Philemon 13–14 | `ἐβουλόμην` is imperfect middle indicative first singular; `κατέχειν` present active infinitive. The speaker wished to retain the male referent so he might serve the speaker on the addressee's behalf. `ἠθέλησα` is aorist active indicative first singular, with aorist active infinitive `ποιῆσαι`; he would do nothing without the addressee's consent. The purpose clause contrasts the addressee's good act seeming compelled with being voluntary. | Swapping who serves whom or whose consent is requested; treating the infinitive as a second finite verb; claiming these two verses explicitly command emancipation or establish a complete position on slavery. Wider historical judgments need wider evidence. |
