## The model and the learning objective

Start from a model that already handles English questions and has useful ancient-language competence. The [large-model comparison](LARGE-MODEL-COMPARISON.md) retains **full Inkling** for the first adaptation experiment, with **Kimi K2.6** as the closest challenger and **Inkling-Small** as the earlier faster/cheaper reference. Kimi often gives better focused explanations, while Inkling completed all 24 questions and offers the stronger practical balance for the first run. The original gpt-oss-120b baseline remains preserved. This is a small diagnostic selection, not demonstrated mastery of biblical languages; all tested models retained material errors. The shortlist and current Meta, NVIDIA, Thinking Machines, and non-U.S. comparisons are in the [model research](model-research.md). Use the completed diagnostic comparison to choose the next experiment, and extend evaluation before public claims; general model benchmark scores cannot make this choice for us.

Training on continuous ancient text uses a next-token prediction objective, often called continued pretraining or domain-adaptive training. In this budget, perform the adaptation through a LoRA adapter rather than updating every parameter. LoRA describes which parameters change; it does not require a question-and-answer dataset.

Tinker accepts token sequences, next-token targets, and per-token training weights. Consequently, its training primitives can optimize directly on the biblical text. Question-answer formatting is needed for the later chat-training comparison, not for all raw-text training. [Tinker quickstart](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/quickstart/), [cross-entropy objective](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/losses/cross-entropy/)

The working hypothesis is that the existing model supplies broad language and reasoning abilities while adaptation strengthens its handling of the selected corpus. What raw-text training explicitly rewards is predicting that text. Better translation, English explanations, or historical interpretation are downstream outcomes to measure, not guaranteed consequences of a lower training loss.

A study of continued document training found that models could improve text prediction while still struggling to answer questions about the documents. Prior instruction tuning helped in that study. This supports starting with a capable instructed model and measuring English access to learned material; it does not establish the best training order for this corpus. [Jiang et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.296/)

Domain adaptation has improved downstream performance in prior research. The foundational study cited here used RoBERTa and classification tasks, so it establishes a precedent for the method, not expected results for a modern Bible assistant. [Gururangan et al., Don't Stop Pretraining](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.740/)

## What “all of it” means for the corpus

Include the entire Hebrew Bible/Old Testament and entire New Testament in the base corpus. Record the precise book inventory and each edition; do not restrict the model to Mark or a few curated passages. Keep Septuagint and deuterocanonical material as separately identified collections, with their source availability and rights recorded before inclusion. This is a scope inventory rather than a claim that every extant manuscript has been collected.

| Layer | Training purpose | Initial decision |
|---|---|---|
| Full Hebrew and Aramaic base text | Exposure to the entire source-language corpus | Use OSHB/WLC with its exact edition and annotations identified |
| Full Greek New Testament | Exposure to a named scholarly Greek edition | Use the current CC BY-licensed SBLGNT release |
| Other ancient versions and manuscript readings | Learn distinctions between forms of a passage | Add permissioned material with witness/edition labels; preserve omissions, gaps, and corrections |
| English translations, glosses, and grammatical information | Strengthen the bridge from ancient wording to English | Keep out of the first raw-text-only experiment; use selected checked examples in a comparison |
| Historical and contemporary ancient-language sources | Supply context beyond the biblical text itself | Add a separately versioned contextual corpus when needed and permitted |

OSHB supplies a Masoretic Hebrew base; it must not be described as the earliest recoverable Hebrew text in every passage. SBLGNT is an edited Greek text, not a single original manuscript. Choosing the closest available text requires editorial judgment and sometimes conflicting evidence. The assistant should retain those distinctions rather than learn an invented unified “original” compiled by the model. [OSHB](https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb), [SBLGNT introduction](https://sblgnt.com/about/introduction/), [SBLGNT license](https://sblgnt.com/license/)

Whole-book coverage is achievable before exhaustive witness coverage. The existing research did not clear a complete commercially reusable Hebrew/DSS/Septuagint apparatus. Track that gap explicitly. Detailed license findings, including the separate rights for annotations and transcriptions, remain in the [source research](source-research.md).

Keep the original text intact, including significant marks and editorial status. Produce separate normalized representations only for search or controlled experiments. Avoid treating a lacuna as omitted wording, or collapsing written/read alternatives into one unexplained string. Give every sequence a source ID, language, work, passage span, and edition/witness label.

Process passages in order within a training window, then shuffle windows appropriately. Do not join unrelated books or manuscript witnesses as if they form a continuous passage. Deduplicate overlapping source distributions, preserve paragraph context, and inspect sampling across Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek so that smaller language portions are not effectively ignored. Any weighting choices are experimental settings, not a new textual-critical method.

There is no need to hand-write answers to every possible Bible question. Nor should an unreviewed model translation become a supposedly authoritative target. A matched English passage must actually correspond to the chosen Greek/Hebrew reading: verse numbers alone do not guarantee alignment. That motivates testing annotations when they help; it does not require building a special translation architecture or prove they improve the shortlisted chat models. ## What success should mean

Evaluate translation adequacy, grammatical interpretation, English-question answering, historical claims, treatment of textual differences, exact quotation, and general instruction-following separately. A fluent English answer can still omit a negation, misread a verb, or introduce a remembered translation that differs from the supplied Greek.

Use two answer modes during testing: questions answered from the model's weights, and questions with the relevant original-language passage supplied. This distinguishes memory/recollection from interpretation of a text in front of the model. Neither mode requires building a RAG system first.

Prepare development and final question sets across the entire corpus. A few passages can be held out of adapter training during model development to test transfer; once the recipe is chosen, a final run may include the whole base corpus. Label the resulting evaluation honestly: new questions about passages the model has trained on are not evidence of unseen-text generalization. The base model may already have encountered biblical passages during its original pretraining, too. Keep final task answer keys out of all training and prompt tuning.

Use the existing [evaluation questions and rubric](evaluation-plan.md) as seeds, extended across languages, books, and genres. Its original retrieval comparison is now an optional additional control. Test short and unfamiliar constructions as well as famous passages; check completion under output limits and compare with the unchanged model on ordinary English instructions.

Watch for overfitting, diminished English usefulness, and a tendency to answer in biblical language or reproduce memorized passages instead of addressing the question. Save checkpoints and prefer modest updates. The exact learning rate, mixture, and epoch count should follow the chosen model's training guidance and measured retention results.

If English performance declines, test a small permissioned English retention mix or a subsequent instruction-training pass. No universal mixture is established here. Research on language adaptation shows that downstream abilities can change in ways not captured by text-prediction loss alone. [ACL 2025 language-adaptation study](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1547/)

The existing model will retain much of its prior training, including modern translations and interpretations. Ancient-only adaptation should not be advertised as removing those influences. Likewise, raw biblical text does not contain all the historical evidence necessary to date its own composition or reconstruct every lost reading. A historically strong model may eventually benefit from a separately curated context corpus.
