# P2 specialist review packet

Prepared September 8, 2026. A private packet now turns the remaining HT01, HT07, and AT03 issues into nine bounded human-review questions. Seven ask for factual adjudication; two separately ask whether the corrected material would be a sound scholarly basis for specified component uses. No reviewer, answer, certification, source promotion, or downstream approval is recorded.

The packet covers Genesis 4:8’s shorter 4Q2 transcription and the Samaritan field invitation; the differing preservation states of אור in three Isaiah 53:11 transcriptions and the contrary originality judgment; and Daniel 2:4b–9’s Hebrew-Aramaic boundary, fragment coverage, Swete Greek edition identities, and plural-form inference limit. Each question names the exact P2 finding IDs, required competence, observed evidence, project inference, expected output, unresolved access, and hash-bound local sources.

The private artifacts are `data/evidence/review-packets/p2-v1/questions.json` and `questions.md`. They remain excluded from publication candidates because they bind unreviewed records and retained research sources. The JSON contains portable parent/follow-up bindings, a deduplicated source register, the question set, and a blank response template. The Markdown is the reviewer-facing rendering.

Use a Hebrew textual critic for HT01 and HT07. Isaiah Q04 additionally needs Septuagint/Isaiah transmission competence. AT03 requires Biblical Aramaic and Greek Daniel textual-transmission competence; more than one reviewer may answer when no individual covers both. Reviewers should cite exact signs, lines, pages, or edition units, supply replacement wording for corrections, disclose qualifications and conflicts, identify any added sources, and state remaining uncertainty. “Outside competence” and “insufficient evidence” are valid outcomes.

Component-use answers address factual fitness and required caveats only. The specialist is not presumed to be a copyright authority. Separate component-level review must decide whether exact excerpts, transcriptions, annotations, scans, authored notes, training examples, and resulting artifacts may be displayed, redistributed, used for training, or released. A favorable specialist answer does not close those decisions.

The source gaps remain explicit: no relevant manuscript image or hand was independently collated; von Gall’s full apparatus and the reported Genesis versions remain unchecked; the Isaiah LXX and Seeligmann article remain uncollated; Tov’s Isaiah PDF was retrieved over unauthenticated HTTP after an HTTPS certificate-name failure; no stable IAA 4Q112 snapshot was retained; and later Greek critical editions and underlying manuscript evidence were not incorporated.
